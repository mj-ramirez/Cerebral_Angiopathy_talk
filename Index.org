* Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
- Cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) is a chronic, degenerative cerebrovascular
  disease caused by deposition of amyloid protein in cerebral vessel walls.
* Criteria for Diagnosis
Charidimou, Boulouis & Frosch et al. (2022) The Boston Criteria Version 2.0 for
Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy: A Multicentre, Retrospective, MRI-neuropathology
Diagnostic Accuracy Study, The Lancet. Neurology. [[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35841910/][PubMed]]
** Clinical Presentation
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Progressive cognitive decline
- Any stroke presentation including Hemiplegia
- Transient focal neurological episodes
*** Transient focal neurological episodes (TFNEs)
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Brief disturbances of motor, somatosensory, visual or language functions
- Frequently recurrent stereotyped episodes of focal disturbances
- Often presenting of transient paresthesia of the mouth or hand
- Frequently combining sensory and motor symptoms
- Very close resemblance to TIAs
- They pose a high risk for ICH and correlate with focal SAHs and cSS
** Imaging
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- CT Head
- MRI
- MR Fast Field Echo magnetic resonance imaging (FFE)
- MR Gradient Echo Sequence magnetic resonance imaging (GRE)
- MR Susceptibility-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (SWI)
- MR Diffusion-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (DWI)
- MR T2-weighted-Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (T2-FLAIR)
*** Imaging features of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Cortico-subcortical microhemorrhages or petecchial hemorrhages (CMB)
- Focal cortical subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH)
- Disseminated cortical superficial siderosis (cSS)
- Lobar ICH with micro bleeds
- Large and confluent areas of white matter hyperintensities in
  T2/FLAIR-weighted images and scattered microbleeds on GRE/SWI sequences
- (Recurrent) lobar ICH - with finger like projections
- White matter hyper intensities (Cortical, Subcortical, Focal)
- Focal Cortical Microinfarcts

** Pathology
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Amyloid-β deposition in cortical/leptomeningeal vessels (intra
  operative or post mortem)
- CSF analysis combined findings of:
  - 50% reduction in levels of amyloid β-42 and β-40 protein
  - Mildly increased total τ(tau)-protein levels
** Genetic
 - Apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 and ε2 allele (risk factor)
 - Family history of CAA or hereditary forms (e.g. Amyloid Precursor Protein
   mutations)
** Supporting Features
Not required but suggestive
- Neuroimaging:
  - Acute convexity subarachnoid hemorrhage (non-traumatic)
  - Severe white matter hyperintensities (posterior-predominant)
  - Enlarged perivascular spaces (centrum semiovale >20 count)
- Genetic:
  - APOE ε4 allele (risk factor)
  - Family history of CAA or hereditary forms (e.g. APP mutations)
** Possible Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
- Age ≥50 years
- MRI evidence of one strictly lobar hemorrhage (ICH, CMB, or cSS)
- No alternative cause identified
** Probable Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
- Age ≥50 years
- MRI evidence of ≥1 of the following
- ≥2 strictly lobar hemorrhages: spontaneous ICH, cerebral microbleeds (CMBs),
  or cortical superficial siderosis (cSS)
- One lobar hemorrhage + focal/disseminated cSS (≥3 sulci)
- Exclusion of other causes (e.g. hypertension, anticoagulation, trauma, hemorrhagic tumor)
** Definite Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy
- Requires postmortem confirmation
- Pathological evidence of amyloid-β deposition in cortical/leptomeningeal vessels
- Documentation of lobar hemorrhage: macro/microbleed, cortical superficial siderosis (cSS), or intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH)
** Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy Related Inflammation
- CAA-RI is often diagnosed in the context of the modified Boston criteria for CAA, which include:
  - Probable or definite CAA based on imaging or pathology.
  - Evidence of inflammation (e.g. white matter changes, edema) not explained by other causes.
- The diagnosis of CAA-RI is based on a combination of clinical, imaging, and sometimes histopathological findings.

** Heamorrhage after Thrombolysis (HAT) Scoring System
3 criteria, 4 points max
- The Points:
  - Prior intra cranial hemorrhage (ICH) = 2 points
  - cortical Superficial Siderosis (cSS) = 1 point
  - APOE e4/e2 = 1 point
- The Interpretation:
  - 0 points = 3.7 risk of bleed
  - 3 points = 25% risk
@@html:<br>@@ Reference: Lou, Safdar & Mehdiratta et al. (2008) The HAT Score: A
Simple Grading Scale for Predicting Hemorrhage after Thrombolysis, Neurology.
[[http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/18955684][PubMed]]

** Antithrombotic Therapy After Intracerebral Haemorrhage
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- RESTART trial: Anticoagulation [[http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/31128924][PubMed]]
  - The notion that anticoagulation should be permanently avoided following ICH
    has been challenged and a more nuanced approach is suggested
  - One such nuance however is that certain phenotypes of CAA confer the highest
    rebleed risk
- Antiplatelets (More controversial)
  - RESTART AP/SoSTART – small and underpowered but initial results are
    appearing no increase in risk. [[https://clinical-brain-sciences.ed.ac.uk/aspiring][ASPIRING]] is recruiting and we are
    participating
  - Metaanalysis suggests modest increase in ICH with antiplatelets with an RR
    1.3-1.5 but is it outweighed by prevention of thrombotic events? JURY IS
    OUT
** Novel treatment modalities
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- Monoclonal Antibodies Targeting Amyloid-β
- β-Secretase (BACE) Inhibitors
- γ-Secretase Modulator
- Amyloid Aggregation Inhibitors
- Active Immunotherapy
* Prognosis and Outcomes
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
- There is no cure
- Managing hypertension and lifestyle modifications can slow progression
- Avoiding anticoagulant to reduce hemorrhage risk
