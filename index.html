Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy

  • Cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) is a chronic, degenerative cerebrovascular disease caused by deposition of amyloid protein in cerebral vessel walls.
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Clinical Presentation

  • Progressive cognitive decline
  • Any stroke presentation including Hemiplegia
  • Transient focal neurological episodes
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Transient focal neurological episodes (TFNEs)

  • Brief disturbances of motor, somatosensory, visual or language functions
  • Frequently recurrent stereotyped episodes of focal disturbances
  • Often presenting of transient paresthesia of the mouth or hand
  • Frequently combining sensory and motor symptoms
  • Very close resemblance to TIAs
  • They pose a high risk for ICH and correlate with focal SAHs and cSS

Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Imaging features of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy

  • Cortico-subcortical microheamorrhages or petecchial heamorrhages (CMB)
  • Focal cortical subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH)
  • Disseminated cortical superficial siderosis (cSS)
  • Lobar ICH with micro bleeds
  • Large and confluent areas of white matter hyperintensities in T2/FLAIR-weighted images and scattered microbleeds on GRE/SWI sequences
  • (Recurrent) lobar ICH - with finger like projections
  • White matter hyper intensities (Cortical, Subcortical, Focal)
  • Focal Cortical Microinfarcts

Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Pathology

  • Amyloid-β deposition in cortical/leptomeningeal vessels (intra operative or post mortem)
  • CSF analysis combined findings of:
    • 50% reduction in levels of amyloid β-42 and β-40 protein
    • Mildly increased total τ(tau)-protein levels
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Genetic

  • Apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 and ε2 allele (risk factor)
  • Family history of CAA or hereditary forms (e.g. Amyloid Precursor Protein mutations)
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Supporting Features

Not required but suggestive

  • Neuroimaging:
    • Acute convexity subarachnoid heamorrhage (non-traumatic)
    • Severe white matter hyperintensities (posterior-predominant)
    • Enlarged perivascular spaces (centrum semiovale >20 count)
  • Genetic:
    • APOE ε4 allele (risk factor)
    • Family history of CAA or hereditary forms (e.g. APP mutations)
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Possible Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy

  • Age ≥50 years
  • MRI evidence of one strictly lobar heamorrhage (ICH, CMB, or cSS)
  • No alternative cause identified
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Probable Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy

  • Age ≥50 years
  • MRI evidence of ≥1 of the following
  • ≥2 strictly lobar heamorrhages: spontaneous ICH, cerebral microbleeds (CMBs), or cortical superficial siderosis (cSS)
  • One lobar heamorrhage + focal/disseminated cSS (≥3 sulci)
  • Exclusion of other causes (e.g. hypertension, anticoagulation, trauma, heamorrhagic tumor)
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Definite Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy

  • Requires postmortem confirmation
  • Pathological evidence of amyloid-β deposition in cortical/leptomeningeal vessels
  • Documentation of lobar heamorrhage: macro/microbleed, cortical superficial siderosis (cSS), or intracerebral heamorrhage (ICH)
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy Related Inflammation

  • CAA-RI is often diagnosed in the context of the modified Boston criteria for CAA, which include:
    • Probable or definite CAA based on imaging or pathology.
    • Evidence of inflammation (e.g. white matter changes, edema) not explained by other causes.
  • The diagnosis of CAA-RI is based on a combination of clinical, imaging, and sometimes histopathological findings.

Heamorrhage after Thrombolysis (HAT) Scoring System

3 criteria, 4 points max

  • The Points:
    • Prior intra cranial heamorrhage (ICH) = 2 points
    • cortical Superficial Siderosis (cSS) = 1 point
    • APOE ε4/ε2 = 1 point
  • The Interpretation:
    • 0 points = 3.7 risk of bleed
    • 3 points = 25% risk


Reference: Lou, Safdar & Mehdiratta et al. (2008) The HAT Score: A Simple Grading Scale for Predicting Heamorrhage after Thrombolysis, Neurology. PubMed

Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Antithrombotic Therapy After Intracerebral Haemorrhage

  • RESTART trial: Anticoagulation PubMed
    • The notion that anticoagulation should be permanently avoided following ICH has been challenged and a more nuanced approach is suggested
    • One such nuance however is that certain phenotypes of CAA confer the highest rebleed risk
  • Antiplatelets (More controversial)
    • RESTART AP/SoSTART – small and underpowered but initial results are appearing no increase in risk. ASPIRING is recruiting and we are participating
    • Metaanalysis suggests modest increase in ICH with antiplatelets with an RR 1.3-1.5 but is it outweighed by prevention of thrombotic events? JURY IS OUT
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Novel treatment modalities

  • Monoclonal Antibodies Targeting Amyloid-β
  • β-Secretase (BACE) Inhibitors
  • γ-Secretase Modulator
  • Amyloid Aggregation Inhibitors
  • Active Immunotherapy
Stroke Unit Broomfield Hospital

Prognosis and Outcomes

  • There is no cure
  • Managing hypertension and lifestyle modifications can slow progression
  • Avoiding anticoagulant to reduce heamorrhage risk
